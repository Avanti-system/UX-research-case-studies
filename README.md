**Project 1: New Product Concept**

Task — Brief

From: Client Product Team (cybersecurity SaaS)

A cybersecurity software client is exploring a new product concept called "GuardianView" — a premium add-on for their core security suite that gives parents real-time visibility into their children's online activity, app usage, and screen time. The client wants to know whether home users with children aged 5–16 would actually pay for this, how they perceive the concept versus doing nothing or using free alternatives, and which features matter most.
The task: design a research approach to validate this concept before the client invests in development.

**INTRODUCTION**

Title New Product Concept — GuardianView

Author - Avanti Thakre · UX Researcher · avanti.thakre92@gmail.com

Stakeholders - Product Manager, Product Team, VP Consumer Businesses

Date - 04/06/2026

Project Background - With children aged 5–16 spending more time online, parents face growing challenges managing screen time, content exposure, and digital safety concerns. To address this gap, the client is exploring a new product concept — "GuardianView" — a premium add-on for their core security suite that gives parents real-time visibility into their children's online activity, app usage, and screen time. This research aims to provide actionable recommendations on whether parents with children aged 5–16 would actually pay for this, and which feature is most in demand.

Research Goals - Identify whether parents will buy a premium add-on for the client's core security product, and among the three candidate features (online activity, app usage, screen time) which drives purchase intent most strongly. Provide insight into whether parents understand the concept and would use it despite free alternatives.

**RESEARCH QUESTIONS**
Q1. Will parents of children aged 5–16 pay for a premium security add-on of this kind?
Q2. Which feature would justify that purchase?

**HYPOTHESIS**
Parents are aware of the risk but manage it passively. They will show interest in the concept but resist paying if free alternatives (Google Family Link, iOS Screen Time) feel good enough. Differentiation must come from integration with the client's existing security infrastructure — not just screen time controls.

**METHODOLOGY**
Phase 1 — Concept test survey (n=350, parents aged 25–45 with children 5–16): Show 3 concept variants of GuardianView differing in feature set and pricing. Measure appeal, purchase intent, uniqueness, and believability (standard concept test scorecard). Use MaxDiff to identify which specific feature drives willingness to pay.
Phase 2 — Van Westendorp pricing model within the same survey, to identify the acceptable, optimal, and ceiling price points for the add-on.
Phase 3 — 6 in-depth interviews (IDIs) with parents who scored high on purchase intent, to understand the emotional drivers behind purchase intent.

**PARTICIPANT / RECRUITMENT**
Parents aged 25–45, primary caregivers, with at least one child aged 5–16 in the household. Currently using a paid antivirus or security software product.

**DELIVERABLE FRAMING**
This research study helps the product team understand whether to build GuardianView, at what price point, and with which feature set as a minimum viable proposition — or whether to redirect the budget to a different growth opportunity.
